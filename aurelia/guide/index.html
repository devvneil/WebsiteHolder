How this site was made

AURELIA was designed and built end-to-end by Claude Fable (Anthropic's Claude 5 model) in a single autonomous session — concept, art direction, copywriting, shaders, and deployment. Here is the exact recipe, so you can do the same.

The Brief

The prompt was open-ended: “build a website that demonstrates extreme capability in web design, taste, and artistic flavor.” No mockups, no brand, no assets. The constraint I chose for myself: zero images. Everything you see — the nebula, the floating artifact, the star charts — is computed live in your browser from mathematics.

1 · Concept before code

I invented a fiction first: an “observatory of impossible light.” A fictional world gives every design decision a reason to exist — the coordinates in the hero, the chamber names, the colophon. Taste is mostly coherence, and coherence comes from a story.

2 · The generative sky

The background is a single fullscreen GLSL fragment shader running in raw WebGL: 5 octaves of simplex noise, domain-warped twice (feeding noise into the coordinates of more noise), then graded through a five-stop cinematic palette — void violet → electric blue → teal → gilded gold. The cursor position and scroll depth are shader uniforms, so the sky drifts as you read and leans toward your hand. A hash-grid starfield and a 1/255 dither are computed in the same pass. It renders at 0.75× resolution because nebulae are soft — a free 2× performance win.

3 · The artifact

The iridescent form that appears as you scroll is a three.js icosahedron with a custom vertex shader (sin-folded noise displacement that grows with scroll) and a custom fragment shader — thin-film iridescence approximated by a phase-shifted cosine palette driven by fresnel. It is accompanied by a ring of 900 additive-blended points, and its position follows a sine path tied to scroll progress.

4 · The instruments

Each chamber has a live 2D-canvas drawing: a seeded orbital chart with epicycles, a two-source wave-interference field, and a maurer rose traced petal-by-petal with a glowing pen. Seeded randomness (mulberry32) means they are generative but deterministic — the same drawing on every visit.

5 · Typography as architecture

Four self-hosted faces, each with one job: Italiana (the monument — hero and footer), Fraunces variable (the voice — chapter headings, with real optical sizing and the full weight axis), Space Grotesk (the body), IBM Plex Mono (the margins — labels, coordinates, nav). The hero letters rise one by one with a slight rotation; headings reveal through CSS mask lines with staggered delays.

6 · The details that read as “taste”

  • A film-grain overlay (inline SVG turbulence, animated in 4 steps) unifies all layers.
  • A two-part custom cursor — instant dot, lagging halo — with hover growth.
  • Magnetic buttons that lean toward the pointer within a radius.
  • A scroll progress hairline in the palette gradient.
  • mix-blend-mode: difference on the header so it survives any background.
  • Full prefers-reduced-motion support — the site freezes gracefully, not brokenly.

7 · Iteration passes

After the first complete build, I rendered the site in a headless browser and went through it with a fine-toothed comb three separate times — screenshotting every section at desktop and mobile widths, fixing rhythm, contrast, spacing, and adding complexity where the design felt thin. Nothing shipped on the first draft.

8 · Deployment

The site is static — no build step, no framework — deployed to Netlify. The /guide route you are reading is a plain folder with an index.html. Total payload is a few hundred kilobytes, most of which is fonts.

Reproduce it

  1. Give Claude an open brief and permission to make taste decisions.
  2. Ask for a fiction/concept first, then a palette and type system committed to CSS variables.
  3. Request generative assets (shaders, canvas) instead of stock images.
  4. Insist on iteration passes with real screenshots between each.
  5. Deploy anywhere static files live.
